# Figures for the tuned Part 2 models (XGBoost, MLP, approximate SVM)

Generated by `scripts/figures_part2/make_parity_and_maps.py` (see
`scripts/figures_part2/README.md`). All numbers come from the out-of-fold
predictions of the Part 2 run (`outputs/bench/parte2/ca706e27654e0cf5/<family>/tuned/oof.parquet`),
LeaveOneShapeOut: each of the 40 hydrograph shapes is predicted by a model that
never saw that shape. 154,080 node-scenario rows (160 nodes x 963 scenarios;
the 963 scenarios are the 40 shapes x 25 factors left after the continuity
filter). Predicted routed volume of a row = regressor output where the
classifier says "flooded", else 0 (the end-to-end prediction the evaluation
scores). The machine-readable copy of every printed number is
`figure_numbers.json`.

Colour language: XGBoost blue, MLP orange, approximate SVM green, SWMM dark grey.

Check against the audited table
(`docs/auditoria/correcciones/datos_parte2/comparacion_protocolos.csv`, column
`rmse_vol_agregado_m3`): the pooled RMSE printed on the parity figure equals it
to four decimals (69.6424 / 101.1170 / 214.8677 m3); the script asserts this.

## Figures

### 1. `parity_nodes_3models.png`

- Shows: predicted vs SWMM routed volume for every node-scenario row, one panel
  per model, 2-D density (white to model colour, log colour scale) on
  log10(1 + volume) axes so zeros and 10,000 m3 fit together. Dashed line y = x.
- How to read: points on the diagonal are perfect. The strip along the bottom
  axis is SWMM-flooded rows the model predicted dry; the strip along the left
  axis is dry rows predicted flooded.
- Numbers printed (source: pooled OOF, the table above): RMSE 69.64 / 101.12 /
  214.87 m3; R2 0.961 / 0.918 / 0.628; n = 154,080 rows; "both zero" 69.1% /
  69.0% / 66.0% of rows.
- What to say:
  - XGBoost and MLP hug the diagonal over four orders of magnitude; XGBoost has
    the lowest RMSE (69.64 vs 101.12 m3).
  - The approximate SVM is visibly wider around the diagonal and has two
    clear failure strips: 9,076 flooded rows predicted dry and 6,117 dry rows
    predicted flooded (XGBoost: 379 and 1,382).
  - RMSE is dominated by the large-volume rows, which are few; the density hides
    that, so quote RMSE together with the scenario-level figure below.
- Limitations: log1p scaling makes small-volume errors look larger than they are in
  absolute terms; the colour is a row count, not a probability; density in the
  low-volume corner is dominated by the many near-zero rows.

### 2. `parity_scenario_totals_3models.png`

- Shows: total flooded volume of each scenario (shape x factor), predicted vs SWMM,
  one marker per scenario (963), log-log axes, three models overlaid, y = x dashed.
- How to read: distance from the dashed line is the relative error of the
  scenario total. Values below 1 m3 are drawn at 1 m3 (the column at x = 1 holds
  the 173 scenarios with zero SWMM volume plus a few tiny ones).
- Numbers printed (source: `figure_numbers.json`, `scenario_median_ape_pct`): median
  absolute percentage error over the 790 scenarios with SWMM total >= 1 m3:
  XGBoost 15.5%, MLP 12.7%, approximate SVM 30.4% (90th percentile: 59.9% /
  51.5% / 156.6%).
- What to say:
  - At scenario level all three follow the diagonal from 10 to 180,000 m3; the
    median error is 13-15% for XGBoost and MLP and 30% for the SVM.
  - At this level the MLP is slightly better than XGBoost on the median (12.7% vs
    15.5%), even though XGBoost is clearly better in pooled node RMSE; the node-level
    RMSE and the scenario-level percentage error answer different questions.
  - The SVM saturates at the top: for the five largest scenarios
    (all `shape_quasi_uniform_block`, factors 4 to 5, SWMM 142,000-183,000 m3) it predicts
    about 67,000-75,000 m3; it also predicts non-zero volume in 102 of the 173 scenarios
    that are dry in SWMM (XGBoost 3, MLP 2).
- Limitations: the median APE is taken over scenarios with SWMM total >= 1 m3; the
  tiny-volume scenarios (left side) have large percentage errors but negligible
  absolute volume; markers overlap in the dense upper-right region.

### 3. `error_by_shape.png`

- Shows: per-shape total flood volume error (%) of each model (sum of predicted
  routed volume over all rows of the shape and all factors, divided by the SWMM
  sum, minus 1), 40 shapes, sorted by absolute XGBoost error (largest at top).
  `common_bogota` and `shape_quasi_uniform_block` are highlighted in red.
- How to read: bars to the right = over-prediction, to the left = under-prediction.
  SVM bars longer than 120% are clipped and the true value is written next to them.
- Numbers printed (source: `figure_numbers.json`, `shapes`): XGBoost errors range
  from -31.2% (`shape_quasi_uniform_block`) to +33.0% (`common_bogota`); the
  median absolute error across shapes is 13.7% (XGBoost), 10.5% (MLP), 20.9% (SVM);
  shapes with |error| > 20%: 7 / 5 / 21. SVM extremes: `shape_ultra_short_impulse`
  +276%, `rare_extreme` +139%.
- What to say:
  - Failures are concentrated in a few shapes, not spread evenly: the worst two
    for XGBoost are the highlighted ones. `shape_quasi_uniform_block` is
    under-predicted by all three (XGBoost -31.2%, MLP -26.3%, SVM -42.9%);
    it carries the largest SWMM volume of all shapes (1.77 million m3 in total),
    so it also dominates the pooled RMSE.
  - `common_bogota` is over-predicted by all three (+33.0% / +61.6% / +93.6%);
    it is a small-volume shape (21,577 m3 in SWMM) so its absolute impact is small.
  - The MLP has the smaller median shape error but a larger worst case than
    XGBoost (+61.6% on `common_bogota`).
- Limitations: the error is pooled over all factors of the shape, so over- and
  under-prediction at different factors can cancel; small-volume shapes give
  large percentages for tiny absolute errors.

### 4. Flood maps: `flood_map_<rule>_<shape>_f<factor>.png`

- Shows: the Chico network, nodes coloured and sized by flooded volume, four panels
  SWMM | XGBoost | MLP | approximate SVM, one common colour scale (square-root
  scale, colour bar "Flood volume (m3)"); dry nodes small and grey; marker outline
  uses the model colour. Under each panel: total volume (and its error vs SWMM),
  number of flooded nodes (+ false positives, - missed) and the node-level RMSE of
  that scenario.
- Scenario selection rule (documented, not hand-picked; error = total squared
  error of tuned XGBoost summed over all rows of the shape, all factors):
  - `max_error`: the shape with the largest error: `shape_quasi_uniform_block`.
  - `median_error`: the shape at position 21 of 40 in the ascending ranking
    (upper median): `shape_delayed_abrupt_peak`.
  - `min_error`: the lowest error among shapes whose displayed scenario has at
    least 20 SWMM-flooded rows: `shape_ultra_short_impulse` (36 flooded nodes).
  - Factor 3.0 for all three; the 3.0 cell exists for every shape, so no
    nearest-factor substitution was needed.

(Source: `figure_numbers.json`, `scenario_stats`; node RMSE in m3 over the 160 nodes.)

- How to read: compare colour and size of the same node across panels; an orange
  or green marker on a node that is grey in the SWMM panel is a false positive.
- What to say:
  - Median scenario: XGBoost and MLP reproduce the spatial pattern node by node
    (same 60 flooded nodes, node RMSE about 20 m3); the SVM misses 10 flooded nodes,
    invents 5, and has a node RMSE about four times larger (89.4 m3).
  - Worst shape: the spatial pattern is right but the magnitude is
    systematically low for all three (-27.7% / -37.1% / -27.4% in total volume);
    XGBoost has the right set of flooded nodes (61, no false, no missed) but
    underestimates the hot spots (for example the largest node, about 6,000 m3 in
    SWMM, is about 4,500 m3 in XGBoost). The SVM misclassifies 20 nodes.
  - Best-error shape: the low squared error is a consequence of tiny volumes
    (283 m3 in total); the maps show it is not an easy scenario in relative terms:
    every model over-predicts (+49% / +88% / +430%) and flags 20-23 extra nodes.
- Limitations: one scenario each, at factor 3.0; the colour scale is shared so
  the small-volume scenario is mostly one colour range; the `min_error` rule picks
  the shape with the lowest absolute squared error, which favours low-volume
  shapes, so it is not a "typical good case". Colours use a square-root scale to
  keep small nodes visible, so colour differences are not proportional to volume.

### 5. Node profiles: `node_profile_<rule>_<shape>_f<factor>.png`

- Shows: for the same three scenarios, grouped bars (SWMM, XGBoost, MLP, SVM) for
  every node flooded in SWMM, sorted by SWMM volume; full node IDs on the axis.
  The title counts the nodes that are dry in SWMM but predicted flooded
  (false positives) by each model.
- How to read: a missing bar means the model predicted 0 for that node.
- Numbers printed: SWMM-flooded nodes 61 / 60 / 36; false positives (XGBoost / MLP /
  SVM) 0 / 4 / 13, 0 / 0 / 5 and 20 / 23 / 22 for the three scenarios in the order
  max, median, min (source: `figure_numbers.json`, `scenario_stats`).
- What to say:
  - Median scenario: bars of XGBoost and MLP track SWMM within about 20% on
    the biggest nodes; the SVM has big gaps (for example node 36C: about 740 m3 vs
    1,120 m3 in SWMM) and large over-predictions on individual nodes (29C, 71C, 27C, 2C).
  - Worst shape: all models under-predict most of the large nodes, XGBoost the
    least; the largest node (about 6,000 m3 in SWMM) gets about 4,500 (XGBoost),
    3,200 (MLP) and 2,100 (SVM).
- Limitations: false-positive nodes are not drawn as bars, only counted in the
  title; node IDs that differ only by suffix (for example `91C`, `91I`, `91Ib`)
  are different nodes.

## Overall message and honest caveats

- Pooled, the ranking is XGBoost (RMSE 69.64 m3) < MLP (101.12) < approximate
  SVM (214.87); the SVM is clearly worse on every figure (R2 0.628, 15,000+
  misclassified rows, 21 of 40 shapes with more than 20% total-volume error, and
  saturation on the largest scenarios).
- The two gradient-based/tree models still have a systematic weak spot:
  `shape_quasi_uniform_block` (-31% XGBoost), which carries the largest volumes
  and is the main contributor to the pooled error. With only 40 shapes, one
  shape held out removes a large part of the high-volume training space.
- At scenario level the MLP has the lowest median percentage error (12.7% vs
  15.5% for XGBoost); the ranking by node RMSE and by scenario-total error
  differs, and the presentation should not claim XGBoost wins on every metric.
- Everything shown is out-of-fold for unseen shapes only (LeaveOneShapeOut);
  it says nothing about unseen factors, networks or storm statistics outside the
  40 hydrograph shapes.
